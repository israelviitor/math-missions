The Singapore Method in 3 Steps

1. Concrete: your child explores the situation with objects, such as play coins or bottle caps.

2. Drawing: your child represents the problem with bars, making the parts and the whole visible.

3. Calculation: only then does your child write the calculation and solve it.

Draw before you solve.Drawing the problem helps your child see which operation to use instead of guessing.

And you? You don’t need to teach the calculation. When your child gets stuck, simply say:

“Show me with a drawing.”

The guide gives you helpful prompts for when your child gets stuck.

For practice, there are 30 mystery missions. Each includes a “draw the problem” step, and each answer reveals a letter in a code word. If a calculation is wrong, the code word won’t make sense—a clue to go back and check the drawing.

Frequently Asked Questions

I’m not good at math. Can I use this?

Yes. The guide is written for parents, not teachers. You don’t have to teach the calculation: ask your child to draw the problem and use the guide’s prompts when they get stuck.

How do I know where to start?

Start with activities your child can already follow with your help. The missions cover basic operations, fractions, measurement, and understanding word problems. Choose a level based on what your child knows, and move forward when they can explain their drawing.

My child’s school uses a different method. Will this be confusing?

No. Bar models help children understand the problem, regardless of how they calculate the answer. Many schools in the United Kingdom already use bar models.

Can I use it with two children?

They can work together, taking turns reading, drawing, and calculating, or each child can complete a mission that matches their current skills.

How long does it take?

The guide takes about 15 minutes to read. Each mission takes 20–35 minutes, completed in one sitting or one stage per day.

Do I need to print in color?

No. Everything is in black and white.
